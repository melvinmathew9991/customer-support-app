Legal removals and intellectual property
Brightstall respects the valid intellectual property (IP) rights of others and expects merchants to do the same. Posting content that infringes someone else's legal rights breaks the Acceptable Use Policy, and Brightstall may remove that content at its discretion.

Brightstall also will not let its processes be abused. Knowingly sending a false or bad-faith notice of IP infringement is against the law, and anyone harmed by such a notice, Brightstall included, may sue for damages and legal fees.

The rest of this section explains how Brightstall handles IP notices and answers common questions.

IP: copyright, trademark and trade dress
IP means things created through creative effort, such as books, text, code, images, videos, logos and slogans. Copyright, trademark and trade dress are three kinds of IP.

What is a copyright?
Copyright protects original works in a tangible form, such as photos or the product descriptions in an online store. In general, protection starts as soon as the work is created, although copyright law differs from one region to another.

What are a trademark and trade dress?
A trademark is a word or symbol that identifies a business or product and sets it apart from others. Trade dress is the look of a product, such as its size, shape, color and texture, when that look sets it apart from other products. Owners of trademarks and trade dress can generally stop others from using similar marks or looks in a way that is likely to confuse customers about who made a product.

Brightstall's copyright policy
Brightstall follows the Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) and handles claims of copyright infringement through its notice and counter notice process:

When Brightstall receives a valid notice of alleged copyright infringement, it generally removes the content in question and tells the affected merchant, through the store admin, which content was removed.
The merchant can then send a counter notice explaining why they believe the content was removed by mistake. If the counter notice is valid, the merchant can generally repost the content after 10 to 14 business days.
Brightstall may close stores that infringe repeatedly, for example a store that has received several infringement notices it did not counter. Brightstall may also disable a store after a single case of willful or egregious infringement.
Knowingly sending a false or bad-faith infringement notice or counter notice is against the law, and anyone harmed by it, Brightstall included, may sue for damages and legal fees.

Reporting copyright infringement
The quickest way to report alleged copyright infringement is Brightstall's online form.

Brightstall can act on a notice only if it contains all of the following:

Your legal name and your contact information.
Links to the original copyrighted work you say is being infringed. If you have no link, a description of the work is enough.
Links to each page on Brightstall that you believe infringes your copyright. You must list every individual page; a link to the store's home page is not accepted.
Confirmation that:
You believe in good faith that the content is being used without the permission of the copyright owner, its agent or the law.
Everything in the notice is accurate.
A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you own the copyright or are authorized to act for its owner.
Your physical or electronic signature.
If you cannot use the online form, you can send a notice containing the same information to Brightstall's designated agent:

Brightstall Trust & Safety

400 Example Avenue, Suite 12
Springfield

Brightstall may give a copy of your notice, including the contact details in it, to the person who posted the content you reported.

FAQ - Responding to a DMCA copyright notice
My store received a DMCA copyright notice. What happens now?

When Brightstall receives a DMCA notice about your store that it reasonably believes is complete and valid, it generally removes the content named in the notice, and you receive a notification in your store admin. You then have two choices:

If you agree after reviewing the notice that you do not have the right to use the content, you do not need to do anything. The content stays offline unless the copyright owner gives you permission to use it.
If you believe the content was removed by mistake or because it was misidentified, you can file a DMCA counter notice.
How do I file a DMCA counter notice?

You send a counter notice through a form in your store admin. Brightstall considers it only if it contains all of the following:

The legal name and enough contact information for the person sending the counter notice.
Which content was removed, and where it appeared before it was removed.
A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you believe in good faith that the content was removed by mistake or because it was misidentified.
A statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the Federal District Court for the judicial district where your address is located, or, if your address is outside the United States, of the Federal District Court for the District of Delaware, and that you will accept service of process from the person who sent the copyright notice or their agent.
Your physical or electronic signature.
If you cannot use the form, you can send a counter notice containing the same information to Brightstall's designated agent at the address above.

What happens after I file a DMCA counter notice?

Once you send a valid counter notice, Brightstall removes the complaint from your account record and sends a copy of your counter notice to whoever sent the original notice. If that person starts legal action against you and tells Brightstall within 10 to 14 business days, the content may stay offline. If Brightstall hears nothing about legal action in that time, it lets you know and you can repost the content.

Brightstall's trademark and trade dress policy
Trademark and trade dress notices are handled much like copyright notices, with some important differences.

When Brightstall receives a valid notice of trademark or trade dress infringement about content in a store, it tells the merchant directly in the store admin which content the notice is about. You have two days to respond before the content is removed.
If Brightstall finds your response sufficient, it may decide not to remove the content, or, if the content was already removed, let you repost it straight away.

Reporting trademark and trade dress infringement
The quickest way to report alleged trademark or trade dress infringement is Brightstall's online form.

Brightstall can act on a trademark or trade dress notice only if it contains all of the following:

Your legal name or your contact information.
Links to examples of the trademark or trade dress you say is being infringed. If you have no link, a description is enough.
Links to each page on Brightstall that you believe infringes your trademark or trade dress. You must list every individual page; a link to the store's home page is not accepted.
For a trademark:
For each trademark involved:
A description of the trademark.
The countries where it is registered.
Its registration number.
The categories of products or services the registration covers.
How you believe the content infringes the trademark.
For trade dress:
For each trade dress involved:
A description of the trade dress, including the features (such as size, shape, color or texture) that make it distinctive.
A statement that the trade dress is non-functional.
How you believe the content infringes the trade dress, including which of its features are similar enough to cause confusion.
Confirmation that:
You believe in good faith that the content is being used without the permission of the trademark or trade dress owner, its agent or the law.
Everything in the notice is accurate.
A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you own the trademark or trade dress or are authorized to act for its owner.
Your physical or electronic signature.

FAQ - Responding to a trademark or trade dress notice
My store received a trademark or trade dress notice. What happens now?

When your store receives a valid trademark or trade dress notice, you get a notification in your store admin telling you which content was reported. You then have two choices:

If you agree after reviewing the notice that you do not have the right to use the content, you do not need to do anything. The content goes offline if Brightstall does not hear from you within two days.
If you believe the content was reported by mistake or because it was misidentified, you can send a response.
How do I respond to a trademark or trade dress notice?

You send your response through your store admin. Brightstall considers it only if it contains all of the following:

The legal name and enough contact information for the person sending the response.
Which content was reported, and where it appears.
A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you believe in good faith that the content was reported by mistake or because it was misidentified.
A statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the Federal District Court for the judicial district where your address is located, or, if your address is outside the United States, of the Federal District Court for the District of Delaware, and that you will accept service of process from the person who sent the trademark or trade dress notice or their agent.
Your physical or electronic signature.

What happens after I send a response?

Once you send a valid response, Brightstall sends a copy to whoever sent the trademark or trade dress notice and generally lets you keep or repost the content.
